# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **A real small market to own first: one campus.** One university's students are easy to find and serve completely. Becoming the default screen on one campus is a big share of a small market, the right starting shape.
- **A possible network effect.** Events, clubs and announcements get more useful as more of a campus joins. Marks and email do not. That layer is the only part that could compound.
- **A weak switching cost.** If students build the habit of checking deadlines here, leaving costs them that habit.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It sits on data it does not own.** Marks, assignments and email live in Microsoft 365 and each university's ERP/LMS. Those owners can cut access, change it or charge for it, and every university runs something different. Whether these systems expose data to third parties at all is unknown and decides whether the product can exist.
2. **It is not 10x better at anything.** "One dashboard instead of several" is incremental convenience, not a breakthrough. No proprietary technology, no stated secret. The university's IT team or its system vendor could ship the same screen by month six, and the university already owns the student relationship.
3. **No distribution path is shown.** B2C at ₹100/month asks students to pay for a wrapper around free university tools; willingness to pay is unknown. B2B means university procurement, with unknown sales cycles and unknown cost per university. There is no answer yet to how the first 100 customers arrive or what each costs.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about how students use university systems that universities and their vendors do not?
- On your own campus, can you legally and technically pull marks and assignments today? Who signs off?
- What would no university IT team be able to copy within a term?
- Which single campus do you take to most of its students first, and how?
- Who pays, and why keep paying once Microsoft or the ERP vendor improves its own app?
- Why now? What changed this year?

Open numbers: competitor offerings and pricing, and which systems Indian universities run (/founder-competitors); student willingness to pay and real pain (/founder-consumer); cost to win one university and per-student economics (/founder-cfo).

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable: PASS. It reopens with proven data access at one campus, most of that campus using it weekly, and a named advantage (owned data, network effect or contract) a university vendor cannot copy quickly.
