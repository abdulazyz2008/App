# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The pitch opens on a real moment, not on a feature list: a rider down in the rain, family unaware, no blood group to hand. The Product lens starts there and works back, so the starting point is right.
- The core interaction can be described in one sentence: "If you crash and don't answer within 10 seconds, your family and an ambulance get your location and medical ID." That is clear, and it is easy to feel.
- The app runs in the background with nothing to open, so it asks almost nothing of the rider day to day. That is the right instinct for someone riding with a phone in a mount.
- The privacy promise (event type and location only, never name or trip) is a deliberate product decision, and it is the kind that builds trust.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** This is two products (CrashEcho and RoadMemory) serving five audiences, with seven links in the chain. RoadMemory is empty in a new city until many riders confirm hazards, so on day one it feels like nothing. The Product lens would cut everything except crash-to-help until that works very well.
2. **False alarms ruin the experience.** If a pothole sets off "Are you okay?", or the family's phones ring while the rider is fine, trust is gone after the first or second time. There is no accuracy data yet. A crash detector people stop trusting is worse than having none.
3. **Seams the team does not control.** The 108 call, "hospital matched to injury", OS limits on background apps, and battery drain are all hand-offs to other systems. The brief gives no detail on any of them, and the product breaks at exactly those points.

**On the business model:** I would back **(b) a per-rider fee paid by fleets and gig platforms**, set up so the rider keeps the crash-SOS experience and the fleet receives crash alerts only, with no tracking. It keeps the product to one user, one moment and one buyer. **(a) freemium** sets up a bad seam, because safety at the moment of a crash should never sit behind a paywall. **(c) insurers** and **(d) cities** turn the product into a data business, which pulls focus back to RoadMemory and puts pressure on the privacy promise. Treat both as later expansions, not starting points. Whether fleets would pay, and how much, is an open question (/founder-competitors for what fleets already offer riders, /founder-cfo for per-rider cost and price).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the one thing? If you could ship only CrashEcho or only RoadMemory, which would you ship?
- Walk me through a rider's first five minutes: install, permissions, adding family, entering medical ID. Where do they give up?
- How often does it fire falsely on a rough road, and what does the rider feel when that happens?
- What happens second by second after the 10 seconds run out, and who picks up?
- How much battery does it use over a 10-hour delivery shift? Would a rider uninstall it because of that? (/founder-consumer)
- If a fleet pays, how do you keep the rider from feeling watched?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Cut to crash-to-help for delivery riders in one city. Park RoadMemory, red zones and city data.
2. Show false-positive and false-negative rates from real rides before raising money.
3. Map every hand-off (108, hospital, family, OS background limits) and show a working, tested path for each one.
4. Sign one fleet pilot on model (b) that has rider-side privacy terms written in.
