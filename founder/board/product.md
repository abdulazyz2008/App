# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The pain is concrete and frequent: a student misses a deadline or exam notice buried in a mail client they don't check. The moment is easy to picture.
- There is a one-sentence product hidden in the brief: "Never miss anything your university tells you." That is clearer than the pitch itself.
- Daily, phone-first use. If it works, the habit sells it.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The experience runs on data the business does not own.** Marks, assignments, materials and mail all come from university ERP, LMS or Microsoft 365 systems UniDash does not control. Each is a seam. One stale due date or wrong CGPA and the student blames UniDash; trust is gone. Whether they expose data to third parties is unknown. If university IT must approve access, B2C depends on a B2B deal anyway. /founder-competitors should map which ERP/LMS vendors run Indian campuses and whether they offer APIs or their own student apps.
2. **No focus.** "All the things they need" and "as many apps as possible" describe a list of integrations, not a product. Nothing has been cut.
3. **The customer is undefined, so the experience is too.** B2C (the student pays ₹100 a month) and B2B (the university pays) are different products with different buyers and onboarding. Until one is chosen, nothing can be designed. Willingness to pay is unknown: /founder-consumer. Whether ₹100 covers integration and support cost per campus is unknown: /founder-cfo.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Say it in one sentence. What is the one thing it must do brilliantly?
- Take me through the first five minutes. How does a student sign in, and what do they see before anything has synced?
- The day it shows a wrong due date, who finds out and who fixes it?
- Which one university and one system first, and have you confirmed you can get the data?
- What are you cutting from the list, and why?
- Who at the university must say yes, and what do they experience?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Cut it to one job: a single feed of deadlines and announcements. No mail reskin and no marks yet.
2. One campus, with access to its data source confirmed in writing before any build.
3. A reliability bar: over one term, no deadline the official source showed that the app missed or got wrong, checked by a small group of real students.
4. Choose B2C or B2B before designing onboarding, based on the /founder-consumer and /founder-cfo answers.
