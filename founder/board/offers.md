# Offers lens
Score: 1/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The dream outcome is vivid: a washer that never needs charging. If it worked, people would feel stupid saying no.
- Washer buyers do spend money and are easy to reach. The market is not what sinks this.
- There is an honest offer nearby: a battery or solar washer for homes without reliable power. It is a different business, but believable.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood is zero, by physics.** Energy conservation: energy out of Battery A = washing work + losses + energy into Battery B. Typical efficiencies: motor ~0.90, generator ~0.90, battery discharge ~0.95, battery charge ~0.95. Best case, with the drum doing no washing at all: 0.90 x 0.90 x 0.95 x 0.95 = 0.73. So B gets at most 73% of what A gave. After five swaps: 0.73^5 = 0.21, so about 79% has become heat. Every joule spent washing is one B never gets. It runs down. It does not recharge.
2. **The batteries are small for the job.** Voltage is not given. At a typical 3.7 V lithium cell: 15 Ah x 3.7 V = 55.5 Wh per battery. At 12 V: 15 x 12 = 180 Wh. Turning a loaded drum for a full cycle commonly takes on the order of 100-300 Wh, and heating water takes far more. One battery may not finish one wash, and the loop adds nothing.
3. **No proof is possible, so no guarantee is possible.** The offer's headline claim cannot survive the first buyer with a power meter. Expect refunds, not a copy problem.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What voltage are the batteries? Has a prototype run a full wash, and what were the measured Wh out of A and Wh into B?
- Who exactly is the buyer, and what does laundry power cost them today? (Open question: no numbers given.)
- Price, channel, budget, team? (None given.)
- What guarantee would you put in writing, and could you honor it?
- Would you sell the honest version instead: a washer charged from the grid or a solar panel, for people with unreliable power?
