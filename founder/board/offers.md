# Offers lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The buyers are easy to find: students sit together in cohorts, group chats and clubs.
- The pain is real, if small. Notices, deadlines and marks are spread across many places.
- The B2B option points at a buyer with money, who signs once and brings thousands of users.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Weak market on the B2C side.** On this lens's four tests, the pain is mild (inconvenient, not painful), the buyer has little money, the buyers are easy to reach, and growth is unknown. Two of four fail. Students already get email, the LMS and the portal at no cost to them, so a paid layer on top of free tools is the hardest sale there is. Willingness to pay ₹100 a month is an open question for /founder-consumer.
2. **Likelihood is close to zero, and out of the founder's hands.** The value depends on reading each university's marks, assignments and mail. If its ERP or LMS has no third-party access, or IT refuses, the dashboard is empty. There is no product, pilot or confirmed integration.
3. **This is a feature, not an offer.** "Everything in one place" is not a dream outcome anyone pays for, and the university already pays for the systems underneath. With no stack, it competes on convenience and price against free apps and whatever those vendors ship next. Competitor pricing is an open question for /founder-competitors.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who feels this badly enough to pay: the student, the registrar, or the dean whose students miss deadlines?
- What would a university measure: fewer missed submissions, fewer helpdesk tickets? Can one pilot show it?
- At one named university, which systems hold marks and assignments, and do they allow outside access? Have you asked its IT team?
- What would you guarantee?
- If the price doubled, would anyone still choose you over the free apps?
- Cost per campus to build and maintain integrations: an open question for /founder-cfo.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
The vote is PASS. These would reopen it:
- Drop B2C. Rebuild the offer for the university, around one outcome it measures (such as missed-deadline rate), with a pilot guarantee.
- One university confirms in writing that its data can be accessed, and a free pilot runs on one cohort.
- Stack beyond aggregation (deadline alerts, announcements with read receipts, an at-risk student flag) so it is more than a nicer inbox.
