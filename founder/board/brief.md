# Board brief: UniDash (working name)

## The idea (from the founder, in their words, condensed)

A software framework / mobile app for university students, especially in India. Launch in India first, then scale to the US.

Students get all university-related information in one dashboard:
- marks and CGPA
- upcoming university events
- all their subjects, with assignments, assignment due dates and course materials
- all their university emails (Outlook / Microsoft 365)
- "all the things they need to function in university"

The plan is to connect Outlook, Microsoft 365 and the other Microsoft work apps, plus "as many apps as possible" that students need.

## The problem as the founder states it

At an average university it is not convenient for a student to check Outlook email every day. The Outlook interface is not suited to young people. Information is spread across many places, so students miss things. The goal is to make university life easier so students can focus on other things such as extracurricular activities and their overall development.

## Business model (undecided)

- Option A (B2C): about ₹100 per student per month, paid by the student.
- Option B (B2B): sell to the university, which pays in bulk for its students.
- Delivered through an app.

## Stage and constraints

- Not built yet. No product, no users, no pilots.
- No budget, team, timeline or technical plan given.
- The founder is a first-year B.Tech student in Hyderabad, India, who also runs other small ventures.

## What the founder wants to know

- Could this work?
- Does it fit only Indian private universities, or also public universities and government colleges?
- How could it be supplied to universities worldwide?

## Open facts nobody has yet

Market size, how many Indian universities use Microsoft 365 versus Google Workspace, which student-information or learning systems (ERP/LMS) they run and whether those expose data to third parties, competitor pricing, customer acquisition cost, and student willingness to pay. These are open questions, not assumptions.
