## What would flip a no

- If our existing ERP vendor partnered with them or offered it as an add-on, and the pilot report showed a clear drop in missed exams that I could put in front of the committee before next year's budget is set.
- A pilot report from a college like ours showing a clear drop in missed submissions, packaged as evidence we can cite under NAAC student support and progression criteria.
- A pilot report from our own batch showing a clear drop in missed submissions, plus a reference from a similar private college that renewed after the first year.
- If another college like ours showed me their missed-submission numbers going down and how that helped their NAAC report on student support, and the pilot needed nothing from my staff except IT approval.
- A pilot report showing missed submissions clearly dropped in our own batch, timed so I can put it into next year's budget proposal, ideally with a lower per-student price or a cap for a college our size.
- Two or three named peer universities already paying for it, plus pilot numbers showing it measurably cut missed submissions in a way I can put in our NAAC self-study report.
- Our ERP vendor confirming they won't build this soon, plus two or three comparable private colleges that have used UniDash for over a year and show the missed-submission drop in a form we could put in our NAAC file.
- A pilot report from a college like ours showing a clear, measurable drop in missed exams and submissions that WhatsApp groups and the ERP portal didn't achieve, priced low enough to sign off without a committee.
- Speaking to deans at two or three comparable Indian private or autonomous colleges already using it, plus a pilot report showing a clear drop in missed submissions without my staff entering anything twice.
- If our own ERP vendor offered it as a module, or if a pilot showed in our own numbers that missed submissions really fell, enough that the Vice-Chancellor or the academic council asked for it.
- A reference from a registrar at a comparable university who has run it for a year with no data incidents, plus a security audit our IT team accepts.
- A security review I can verify: least-privilege read-only M365 permissions documented, data hosted in India, a third-party audit or pen-test report, and a reference from another university already running it.
