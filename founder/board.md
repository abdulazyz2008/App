# Board verdict: UniDash (working name)

## The vote

**2 PASS, 1 FUND IF.** Average score **3.3 / 10**.

| Lens | Score | Vote |
|---|---|---|
| Offers | 3 | PASS |
| Monopoly | 3 | PASS |
| Product | 4 | FUND IF |

## Risks more than one member raised (most dangerous first)

1. **It runs on data it does not own** (all three). Marks, assignments, materials and email live in Microsoft 365 and each university's ERP/LMS. If those systems give no third-party access, or university IT refuses, the dashboard is empty. Market research confirms that Microsoft Graph access to university mail often needs tenant admin consent, so even the B2C route depends on the university saying yes.
2. **B2C at ₹100/month is the weakest path** (all three). Students already get email, the LMS and the portal free. Paying for a wrapper on top of free tools is the hardest sale.
3. **Not 10x better / a feature, not a business** (Offers, Monopoly). "Everything in one place" is incremental convenience, and the university's own vendor (ERP, LMS, Microsoft) can ship the same screen.
4. **No focus and no chosen customer** (Product, Monopoly). "As many apps as possible" is a list of integrations, not a product. B2C and B2B are different products.

## Where the members disagree

- The **Product lens** sees a real, frequent pain (a missed deadline or exam notice) and a clear one-line product: "Never miss anything your university tells you." It would fund a narrow version.
- The **Offers and Monopoly lenses** pass because the pain is mild, the buyer (the student) has little money, and there is no defensible advantage yet.
- Only the **Monopoly lens** sees a possible network effect, in events, clubs and announcements (not marks or email).

## Conditions checklist (to reopen or fund)

- [ ] Pick one buyer. The board leans towards the university (B2B), not the student. (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- [ ] One named university confirms in writing that its data can be accessed (Microsoft 365 tenant consent and ERP/LMS access) before any build.
- [ ] Cut to one job: a single feed of deadlines and announcements. No email reskin and no marks in version 1.
- [ ] A free pilot with one cohort for one term.
- [ ] Reliability bar: over one term, no deadline shown by the official source is missed or shown wrong.
- [ ] A measured outcome the university cares about (for example, missed-submission rate or helpdesk tickets) and a pilot guarantee built around it.
- [ ] Most of the pilot cohort uses it weekly.
- [ ] A named advantage a university vendor can't copy quickly: owned data such as clubs and events, a network effect, or a contract.
- [ ] Open numbers: cost to win and integrate one university, and per-student economics. (/founder-cfo)

## The strongest version the board can see

This is **not** what was pitched. Don't build a paid all-in-one student app. Instead, build a **free-to-students "never miss a deadline or notice" feed that a university pays for**, starting with one campus where the founder can get the data approved, ideally their own. Version 1 does one thing: it pulls official deadlines, exam notices and announcements into one reliable phone feed with alerts. The university gets something it can measure, such as fewer missed submissions, announcements with read receipts and early flags for students falling behind. The part nobody else owns is the campus layer: clubs, events and student-run notices. That is where a network effect could grow. Marks, CGPA and email come later, through the university's own integrations, once there is a contract.
